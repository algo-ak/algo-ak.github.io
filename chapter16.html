Chapter 16

NP-Completeness

Complexity classes separate problems by what can be solved efficiently, what can be verified efficiently, and what is at least as hard as every problem in NP. The key tool connecting these classes is polynomial-time reduction.

Interactive chapterChange a Boolean assignment, evaluate a 3-CNF formula, and step through the logic of a reduction. Definitions distinguish decision problems from their optimization versions.

16.1Decision and optimization problems

Decision problem

Asks a yes/no question: “Does this array contain a duplicate?” or “Is there a tour of length at most K?”

Optimization problem

Asks for the best feasible value or solution: “Find the minimum-length tour.”

Why decision form mattersP and NP are formally classes of decision problems. An optimization problem is often studied through a related threshold decision problem.

3-CNF as a decision problem

A literal is a variable or its negation. A clause is an OR of literals. A CNF formula is an AND of clauses; in 3-CNF, each clause has exactly three literals.

(¬x ∨ y ∨ z) ∧ (x ∨ ¬y ∨ w) ∧ (¬z ∨ u ∨ v)

The output is YES if some assignment satisfies every clause; otherwise it is NO. The circuit diagram in the slides implements the same Boolean decision: OR gates form clauses and an AND gate combines their outputs.

Precision“Hard” does not simply mean “known to take exponential time.” For many NP-complete problems, we do not know a polynomial-time algorithm, but we also cannot prove that every algorithm must take exponential time. That uncertainty is tied to P versus NP.

16.3P and NP

PDecision problems solvable by a deterministic algorithm in polynomial time.
NPDecision problems for which a proposed YES-certificate can be verified in polynomial time. Equivalently, solvable in polynomial time by a nondeterministic machine.

Every problem in P is also in NP: if a problem can be solved quickly, a proposed answer can also be checked quickly.

16.4What makes a problem NP-complete?

NP-complete = in NP + NP-hard
  • Its YES-solutions are verifiable in polynomial time.
  • Every problem in NP can be reduced to it in polynomial time.
  • No polynomial-time algorithm is known for any NP-complete problem.
  • If one NP-complete problem has a polynomial-time algorithm, then every problem in NP does—and P=NP.
Status we know

NP-complete problems are mutually connected through polynomial reductions and are the hardest decision problems inside NP.

Status we do not know

Whether an NP-complete problem can be solved in polynomial time.

An NP problem not known to be in P or NP-complete: graph isomorphism is a standard example. Integer factorization is another commonly discussed candidate.

16.53-SAT: verify an assignment

Given an assignment, evaluating all clauses is polynomial, so 3-SAT is in NP. 3-SAT is also NP-hard by polynomial-time reductions; therefore it is NP-complete.

Why 3-SAT is NP-complete

The Cook–Levin theorem established SAT as NP-complete. A further polynomial transformation converts SAT instances to equivalent 3-SAT instances. Hence every problem in NP can be reduced to 3-SAT in polynomial time.

16.6Polynomial-time reductions

A ≤p BA polynomial-time reduction transforms every instance x of problem A into an instance f(x) of problem B such that x is a YES-instance of A exactly when f(x) is a YES-instance of B.

Read the direction correctly

To prove B is hard, reduce a problem already known to be hard to B.

instance x

→

ConsequenceIf A≤pB and B has a polynomial-time algorithm, then A also has a polynomial-time algorithm. If A is NP-complete and B∈NP, this proves B is NP-complete.

16.8NP-hard problems

NP-hardA problem H is NP-hard if every problem in NP reduces to H in polynomial time. H need not be a decision problem, need not belong to NP, and need not have polynomially verifiable solutions.
Inside NP

Every NP-complete problem—including 3-SAT—is also NP-hard and has polynomially verifiable YES-certificates.

Outside or beyond NP

The halting problem is undecidable and NP-hard under suitable reductions; optimization TSP and optimization 0/1 knapsack are NP-hard rather than decision problems.

Important correctionNP-hard does not mean “not verifiable in polynomial time.” Verification is simply not required by the definition. Some NP-hard problems are NP-complete and therefore are verifiable in polynomial time.
